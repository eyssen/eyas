# Competitive Analysis

## Analysis Framework

### 1. Identify Competitors
- **Direct:** same problem, same audience (e.g., Notion vs. Coda)
- **Indirect:** same problem, different approach (e.g., Notion vs. pen and paper)
- **Potential:** adjacent products that could expand into your space

### 2. Feature Matrix
Create a comparison table:
| Feature | Our Product | Competitor A | Competitor B |
|---------|------------|-------------|-------------|
| Feature 1 | Yes | Partial | No |
| Pricing | Free tier + paid | Paid only | Freemium |
| Self-hosted | Yes | No | Yes |

### 3. SWOT Analysis
For each competitor:
- **Strengths:** what they do better
- **Weaknesses:** where they fall short
- **Opportunities:** gaps they haven't addressed
- **Threats:** their advantages that endanger our position

### 4. Positioning Map
Plot competitors on two axes relevant to your market:
- Price vs. feature richness
- Ease of use vs. power
- Self-hosted vs. cloud-native
- SMB-focused vs. enterprise-focused

## Data Sources
- Product websites and documentation
- Pricing pages and plan comparisons
- User reviews (G2, Capterra, Product Hunt)
- Job postings (reveal technology and team focus)
- GitHub activity (for open-source competitors)
- Social media and community discussions
- Quarterly earnings reports (public companies)

## Actionable Outputs
- Feature gap analysis: what competitors offer that we don't
- Differentiation strategy: what makes us unique
- Pricing strategy: how to position in the market
- Marketing messaging: counter-positioning against competitors
- Roadmap input: features to prioritize based on competitive gaps

## Update Cadence
- Major review: quarterly
- Pricing/feature monitoring: monthly
- New competitor scanning: ongoing
