# OKRs and KPIs

## OKR Framework

### Structure
- **Objective:** qualitative, inspirational goal (what we want to achieve)
- **Key Results:** 2-5 measurable outcomes per objective (how we know we achieved it)

### Example
**Objective:** Deliver a world-class onboarding experience
- KR1: Reduce time-to-first-value from 15 minutes to 3 minutes
- KR2: Increase onboarding completion rate from 40% to 75%
- KR3: Achieve NPS score of 50+ for new users in first week

### OKR Rules
- Set quarterly (with annual strategic OKRs)
- 3-5 objectives per team, 2-5 key results each
- 70% achievement is a success (stretch goals)
- Score 0.0 to 1.0 at end of quarter
- OKRs are NOT tied to compensation
- Cascade: company → team → individual (aligned, not identical)

## KPIs (Key Performance Indicators)

### Product KPIs
- DAU/MAU (Daily/Monthly Active Users)
- Retention rate (Day 1, Day 7, Day 30)
- Churn rate
- NPS (Net Promoter Score)
- Feature adoption rate

### Engineering KPIs
- Deployment frequency
- Lead time for changes
- Mean time to recovery (MTTR)
- Change failure rate
- Code coverage trend

### Business KPIs
- MRR/ARR (Monthly/Annual Recurring Revenue)
- Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)
- Lifetime Value (LTV)
- LTV:CAC ratio (target > 3:1)
- Burn rate and runway

## OKR vs. KPI
- OKRs are time-bound goals for change and improvement
- KPIs are ongoing health metrics to monitor continuously
- Use KPIs to identify areas that need OKRs
- A KPI going off-track may trigger a new OKR

## Review Cadence
- Weekly: check-in on key result progress
- Monthly: mid-quarter review, adjust tactics
- Quarterly: score OKRs, set new ones, review KPIs
